Address stains as soon as practical after the celebration, including marks that are not immediately visible.

Before long-term storage, ask a specialist to assess lace, beads and layered fabrics. Use archival-quality materials where available and avoid damp storage spaces.

## Inspect after the celebration

Make a note of makeup, food, drink and hemline marks. Some spills appear faint at first and become more noticeable over time. Photograph delicate decorations and any existing damage before sending the gown for treatment.

## Choose a specialist assessment

Wedding gowns can combine satin, tulle, lace, boning, glued embellishments and several layers. One cleaning method may not suit every component. Ask how the cleaner handles trims, colourfastness and any visible staining.

## Preserve without trapping moisture

Only store a completely dry gown. Use clean, suitable archival materials where available and keep it in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight. Inspect periodically rather than assuming storage is maintenance-free.

## When to ask a professional

Garment labels, dyes, embellishments and previous cleaning treatments can change the safest approach. If an item is valuable, delicate or heavily stained, seek a garment-specific assessment rather than experimenting with harsh chemicals or heat.
