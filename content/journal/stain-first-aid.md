Act quickly, but resist the urge to rub. Blot excess liquid with a clean cloth and check the garment's care instructions before applying water.

Different stains require different treatments. Heat can permanently set some stains. Tell your cleaner what caused the mark and which home treatments you already tried.

## First, identify the garment

Read the care label before adding water or cleaning products. Silk, wool, rayon, leather and embellished fabrics can react badly to treatments that seem harmless on ordinary cotton.

## Blot, never rub

Lift any solid residue gently, then blot excess liquid from the outside of the stain toward its centre using a clean white cloth. Rubbing can spread the stain and damage fibres. Avoid hot water until you know the stain type.

## Know when to stop

Do not mix household chemicals or use bleach on an unknown fabric. Heat from ironing or tumble drying can set some stains permanently. If the mark remains, stop experimenting and tell the cleaner what caused it and what you have already applied.

## When to ask a professional

Garment labels, dyes, embellishments and previous cleaning treatments can change the safest approach. If an item is valuable, delicate or heavily stained, seek a garment-specific assessment rather than experimenting with harsh chemicals or heat.
