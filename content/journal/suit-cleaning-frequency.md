Suits do not necessarily need dry cleaning after every wear. Airing, gentle brushing and prompt attention to spills can help preserve fabric.

Cleaning frequency depends on use, perspiration, visible soiling and the manufacturer's instructions. Overcleaning may shorten the life of some fabrics.

## Cleaning depends on wear

A suit worn briefly in a cool environment may only need airing and brushing. A suit exposed to sweat, food spills, smoke or visible dirt may need attention sooner. Always follow the care label and assess the condition rather than relying on a fixed number of wears.

## Protect the shape

Hang jackets on wide, shaped hangers and allow them to air between wears. Empty pockets and avoid overcrowding your wardrobe. Use a soft garment brush where suitable and spot-clean only when the fabric permits.

## Use professional judgement

Dry cleaning is useful for many tailored garments, but repeated unnecessary processing can affect finishes and structure. Ask the cleaner to inspect stains, lining and trims before treatment.

## When to ask a professional

Garment labels, dyes, embellishments and previous cleaning treatments can change the safest approach. If an item is valuable, delicate or heavily stained, seek a garment-specific assessment rather than experimenting with harsh chemicals or heat.
